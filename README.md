# Audio Interface UX Curriculum

A practical, self-paced curriculum for researching, designing, and prototyping the controls and screens of physical audio products: synthesizers, pedals, mixers, controllers, recorders, and similar instruments.

The curriculum connects interaction design with the realities of making sound. You will learn to map signal flow, design legible control systems, prototype physical and digital interactions, and test them with musicians. No electronics or music-theory background is required; optional hardware and software paths are included.

## Start here

1. Read the [curriculum roadmap](curriculum.md) and choose a product type and learning track.
2. Keep a design journal: record observations, assumptions, decisions, and test results.
3. Work through the [project briefs](projects.md) as you learn. Each project builds toward a final prototype.
4. Use the [resource guide](resources.md) selectively; it is a reference shelf, not a prerequisite reading list.

## Learning outcomes

By the end, you should be able to:

- Explain a product's users, context, signal flow, and core tasks.
- Turn research into task flows, control maps, and interaction requirements.
- Design coherent physical controls, screen layouts, feedback, and system states.
- Prototype and test an interaction without needing a production-ready instrument.
- Evaluate accessibility, error recovery, discoverability, and performance on stage or in a studio.
- Present a traceable rationale from research through iteration.

## Suggested working rhythm

For each module, learn the concepts, apply them to a project artifact, and get feedback from another person. Keep artifacts lightweight: sketches, paper controls, clickable screens, annotated photos, or a simple hardware mock-up are all valid prototypes. Test the interaction, not the polish.

## Scope and safety

This is a design curriculum, not a substitute for electrical engineering, product compliance, or hearing-safety training. Keep early prototypes low-voltage and non-audio where possible. Do not connect improvised circuits to mains power, valuable instruments, or loudspeakers. Use headphones at a safe listening level and provide an accessible, non-audio way to understand important status changes.

## Contribution

You can adapt the sequence to a class, a personal project, or a team workshop. When adding resources or exercises, prefer durable references, state who they help, and avoid presenting a specific tool or music genre as universal.
